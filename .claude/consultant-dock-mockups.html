Consultant page · mobile bottom bar

Bigger buttons, an iOS feel — and a cart that knows its place

Three directions for the consultant page's bottom controls. All of them make the primary actions thumb-sized instead of two mid-size pills floating in the middle of the bar, and each one answers the "cart bubble feels off" problem differently. Phone frames use the app's real Georgian strings and light theme.

Use the Active order toggle on each phone — the cart treatment is the main difference between options.

Diagnosis

Why it feels off

What the current layout does that reads as "web page", not "app".

  • The most-used control on the page — Scan — is a 46 px pill capped at 200 px, floating centered with dead space either side. iOS puts the primary action edge-to-edge at 50–56 px.
  • The bar stacks two rows (~112 px tall), yet nothing inside it is actually big. Height without size.
  • The cart bubble hovers 130 px up the screen: it covers list rows, warehouse cards and drawer buttons as you scroll.
  • One order is announced twice — a blue strip at the top of the page and a bubble at the bottom right. Two homes for the same fact.
  • With no order the bubble goes gray but stays tappable — and tapping it opens the client picker. Nothing about a gray cart says "start an order here".

Directions

Option A

Full-width split bar

Smallest change: Scan takes the whole row as a 56 px squircle, Search becomes a square glass icon button. Cart bubble stays, but only while an order is active.

Recommendation

Option B. It is the only direction that answers both complaints at once: the buttons become genuinely big (full-width 56 px scan, square glass search), and the cart stops being a bubble that floats over content — it becomes a mini-player strip that shows the client, the count and the total exactly where the thumb already is. It also removes the duplicated order indicator at the top of the page instead of keeping two homes for one fact.

If you want the smallest possible diff first, A ships the big buttons alone and can grow into B later. C is the most striking and most "native" silhouette, but it trades away the visible client name and total, and costs the biggest rewrite.
